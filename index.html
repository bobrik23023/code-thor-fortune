How the peg pyramid works

The board is built as a triangle of staggered pegs, with each row wider than the one above it. A ball released from the top has an even chance of going left or right at each peg, and that pattern repeats all the way down. After the last row, it lands in one of the pockets at the base — one more pocket than the number of rows on the board.

Because every bounce is effectively a fifty-fifty split, most balls drift toward the centre, while only a few end up in the outer pockets. Mathematically, the layout behaves like a Galton board, with the results forming a binomial curve. The payout table simply flips that curve around: common central pockets pay less, and rare edge pockets pay more. That is the same logic used across many plinko thorfortune casino games.

Multipliers, odds, and the house margin

Each pocket value is set roughly in line with its probability, then trimmed by a small house margin. Six useful terms to know

Pocket
The slot at the bottom of the board where the ball lands and the multiplier is recorded.
Rows
The number of peg lines the ball passes through; more rows mean more pockets and sharper odds.
Risk level
A preset that spreads or concentrates the payout values without changing the long-run return much.
House margin
The small difference between the fair odds and the payout table that supports the operator.
Provably fair
A verification method that uses published hashes so players can check the result after the round.
Variance
The size of the swings around the average result, which is what the risk setting mainly changes.
